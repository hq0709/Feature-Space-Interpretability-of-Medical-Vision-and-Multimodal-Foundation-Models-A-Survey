Table~\ref{tab:applications} maps the uses the core studies state to the family and locus they rest on, with
the maturity we read from them: \emph{retrospective}, shown on a medical foundation model with a task-level
end-point; \emph{emerging}, shown on general models or as a proof of concept in medicine; \emph{proposed},
argued but not demonstrated. The uses come from the studies' own stated applications and the work they cite,
so this section is a purposive narrative rather than a protocol-bounded review.

In this purposive reading, detection has transferred more consistently than mitigation, a hypothesis the uses below suggest rather than a protocol-bounded comparison. Representation-level evidence supports shortcut and
bias auditing, through proxy groups for missing protected attributes, concept ranking and head-level
localisation~\cite{queiroz2024using,bahre2025underdiagnosis,bhattacharyya2025conformal,yang2024demographic,brown2022detecting,yang2024the,janizek2026visual,yasser2026locating};
grounding and hallucination diagnosis from internal states, by finding-level risk scores, token-time probes
and decoding-time feature
suppression~\cite{hardy2024rextrust,yu2026realtime,nooralahzadeh2026universal,li2026suppressing,phukan2024beyond,li2025the,kossen2024semantic};
shift detection and deployment monitoring, since embeddings encode site, scanner and acquisition
(\S\ref{sec:geometry},
\S\ref{sec:decoding})~\cite{dejong2025current,mishra2025comparing,donle2026featmap,disalvo2026mars,poceviciute2024outofdistribution,schulthess2025anomaly,das2026proton};
privacy, memorisation and data auditing, where image--report correspondence in joint spaces creates
re-linkage
risk~\cite{tayebiarasteh2026crossmodal,tonekaboni2025an,deo2026a,hardan2025forgetmi,nasirigerdeh2024machine,zhang2026auditing,xu2026a};
and cheap probes for model selection, training monitoring and data
curation~\cite{liang2026cheap,chen2025revisiting}.

Mitigation and behaviour control remain emerging and model-specific. Attribute removal, feature separation
during adaptation, spurious-behaviour revision and latent steering are each demonstrated
once~\cite{zheng2025towards,huang2025knowledgeguided,pahde2025ensuring,ahsan2025can}, as are causal-tracing
guided fine-tuning, adapter guidance and inference-time activation
addition~\cite{abbasi2026layerspecific,sadanandan2026mechanistically,farina2026translating}; against them,
decodable concepts may be uncontrollable~\cite{basu2026interpretability} and steering may compromise
safety~\cite{korznikov2025the,li2026analysing} (\S\ref{sec:intervention}).

Prospective evidence is missing, of the kind DECIDE-AI specifies for the early-stage clinical
evaluation of decision-support systems~\cite{vasey2022decide} and FUTURE-AI for deployable medical
AI~\cite{lekadir2023future}: no study reviewed here reaches that stage.
Shortcut audits must show that removing an encoded attribute changes a clinical error rate, not a fairness
metric; hallucination detection needs cross-model
transfer; debugging needs the controls of \S\ref{sec:intervention}; monitoring needs signals calibrated
to action thresholds; and privacy auditing needs a standard re-identification protocol.
Regulatory and clinician-facing use is not yet an application: approved-device audits find sparse
demographic and performance transparency~\cite{muralidharan2024a,mehta2025evaluating}, regulators
identify concepts for adaptive oversight~\cite{warraich2024fda}, reader studies remain rare
(\S\ref{sec:eval-bench}), and no evidence chain links a representation-level measurement to a clinical
decision (\S\ref{sec:future}).
