\textbf{Eligibility and coding.} A \emph{core study} (i) analyses or intervenes on an internal representation
(\S\ref{sec:prelim}) of (ii) an eligible medical vision or vision--language foundation model, or an
encoder used to assemble one (\S\ref{sec:models}), and (iii) reports a measurement. General-domain
methods, datasets, benchmarks, model papers and surveys are contextual. Output-only and
input-attribution-only studies are excluded unless attribution tests a representation-level claim, as
are standalone clinical language models, non-imaging modalities and ineligible task-specific
networks. Model and training-method papers whose representation measurement is instrumental (an
attention map as qualitative evidence, an audit selecting heads for fine-tuning) are
contextual; the tracing count is conditional on this rule (\S\ref{sec:tracing}).

Claims were coded by family, sub-group, locus, concept provenance, evidence type, models, modality,
same-data medical-versus-general comparison, evaluation on more than one model or dataset and, for
interventional claims, matched control (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}); the field definitions are in supplement
S2--S3. A record is one study $\times$ family $\times$ evidence type $\times$ locus, so outcomes sharing all
four components form one record; each carries a stable identifier and is listed in supplementary
Tables~S1--S6. It is not an atomic
proposition: one record can span several concepts, models, datasets or end-points, and a test recorded on it
need not cover every outcome inside it, which \S\ref{sec:findings} bounds outcome by outcome. Record counts
are counts of coded rows; the study and distinct-first-author counts beside them are the less aggregated
quantities. Full texts were read for all but four core studies, which are coded from abstracts and counted
per finding in supplementary Tables~S9--S11. Publication status is not an eligibility criterion: preprints,
theses and repository deposits with a retrievable full text are coded like refereed papers, counted as
preprints, and separated in the refereed-only sensitivity of \S\ref{sec:findings}. Four parameter-editing
and unlearning benchmarks that report only output-level reliability, locality and generality are contextual,
since they identify no representation-level mediator. Frozen-feature benchmarks form a second eligibility
stratum, admitted only when they report a representation-level property beyond task accuracy (supplement
S2); accuracy leaderboards are contextual. The original searches recorded no exclusion reason per record,
which is one of the two defects the frozen protocol of Fig.~\ref{fig:flow} was written to remove.

\textbf{Retrieval.} The corpus was assembled between 15 and 18 August 2026 (publication cutoff 16
August) in the stages of Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}. Searches on the Semantic Scholar Graph API, OpenAlex and
the arXiv API and an unlogged forward pass from method seeds came first. Five logged citation-chaining
waves (backward and forward snowballing~\cite{wohlin2014guidelines}), seeded from the model papers of Table~\ref{tab:models} and from the core studies then known,
fetched \chainFetchedAll{} records, screened \chainScreenedAll{} and added \chainStudiesAdded{} core
studies (\Nstagechain{} after the later benchmark-tier recode). Because several original query strings
and all exclusion reasons had not been preserved, a frozen search protocol with archived query strings was
then run and added \frozenCoreCoded{} more. Finally, a direct MEDLINE/PubMed protocol, written out before
its first request and run on 21 August 2026, retrieves \pmRecall{} of the \pmCoreTotal{} core studies
(MEDLINE indexes few of the preprints) and adds none (supplement S1j). Supplement S1 holds the per-stage
counts and ledgers, with a decision and reason for every screened record.

\textbf{Replayability.} The analysis set is the whole coded corpus, \Ncore{} studies and \Nclaims{}
records, and every count in this article is computed on it. \Nrepstudies{} of the \Ncore{} studies are
returned by a logged, replayable stage: \Nfrozenret{} by the frozen search and \Nchainrec{} more by a
logged chaining fetch seeded from the \Nmodelseeds{} model papers of the fixed seed list or from a study
already so returned, taken to closure (\texttt{notes/strata.json}); the remaining \Nlegacystudies{} are
reachable only through the original searches whose query strings were not preserved. Restricting the
analysis to the replayable \Nrepstudies{} drops a record from \Frepdrop{} of the \Fnrows{} evidence
statements and changes \Freptierw{} corpus-breadth tier (supplement S6). Replayability is provenance
coverage of the assembled corpus, not recall against the eligible literature: the keyword protocol misses
\Nfrozenmissed{} studies already known to us, of which \Fnokw{} contain no term of any of the eight family
query groups, being benchmarks and audits eligible for the property they report rather than for a method
they name, and \Fcapped{} match a group but fall outside a relevance-ranked cap (supplement S1k), and
nothing here estimates how many eligible studies no stage reached.

Screening retained \Nretained{} candidate references (study flow in S0). The article cites \Ncited{}:
\Ncore{} core studies, coded and tabulated, and \Ncontext{} contextual references. \Ncitedcorpus{} of
those citations come from the screened set; the remaining \Ncitedhand{} are entries added by hand ---
regulatory documents and technical reports that no database search returns --- so \Ncited{} is not a
subset of \Nretained{}. Of the core studies,
\Nrecent{} (\Pctrecent\%) appeared in 2025--2026, and 2026 is a partial year (Fig.~\ref{fig:corpus}a).

\textbf{Distribution of the corpus, and what it is not.} Fig.~\ref{fig:corpus} shows where the evidence
sits: observational claims at the vision encoder and in the projected joint space dominate, pathology and
chest radiography supply nearly two thirds of the records, and the few interventional claims divide between
the decoder residual stream and post-hoc operations on encoder or joint-space embeddings. Only one record
repeats a probe at successive loci~\cite{zhu2026lost} and one is taken at the text
encoder~\cite{tayebiarasteh2026self}; none is taken at the connector output alone or at the pre-readout
representation, a sparseness taken up in \S\ref{sec:future}. This is a survey with a protocol-bounded
evidence map of an assembled corpus: a scoping review in the sense of Arksey and
O'Malley~\cite{arksey2005scoping,levac2010scoping,peters2020scoping} with a descriptive synthesis in the
sense of SWiM~\cite{campbell2020swim}, not a systematic review designed or reported to PRISMA, PRISMA-S or
PRISMA-ScR~\cite{page2021prisma,rethlefsen2021prismas,tricco2018prismascr}, and its search strategy has not
been peer reviewed in the sense of PRESS~\cite{mcgowan2016press}. No effect is pooled and no
certainty is graded, so every count below is a description of this corpus, not an estimate of the
literature, and an empty cell in Fig.~\ref{fig:corpus}b means ``no study found under this protocol'', not
that none exists. Four limitations bound it. Retrieval, screening and coding were performed in a single
pass with large-language-model assistance (see the declaration at the end), a form of review automation
whose error profile is not yet established~\cite{oconnor2019question,schoot2020open}, so no inter-coder
reliability is reported. IEEE Xplore and the ACM Digital Library were reached only through the indices above, MEDLINE
having since been searched directly (S1j). No formal risk-of-bias appraisal was applied beyond the
evidence-type, validity-test and quality coding, neither at the review level (ROBIS~\cite{whiting2016robis})
nor at the study level (PROBAST and PROBAST+AI~\cite{wolff2019probast,moons2025probast}, TRIPOD+AI~\cite{collins2024tripod},
QUADAS-2~\cite{whiting2011quadas}): adapting either instrument to representation-level claims would be a
new, unvalidated instrument applied by one coder, so what is reported instead is the domain-by-domain audit
of Fig.~\ref{fig:findings}, and the released per-record fields let a reader apply a PROBAST+AI domain
judgement to the sheet. And the experimental-use section (\S\ref{sec:applications}) and the maturity and
failure-mode cells of Table~\ref{tab:famreq} are the authors' reading rather than coded fields.
