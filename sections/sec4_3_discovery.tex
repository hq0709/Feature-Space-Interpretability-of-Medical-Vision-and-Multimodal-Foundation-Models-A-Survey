\subsection{Feature decomposition and discovery}
\label{sec:discovery}

Discovery decomposes representations into neurons, factorised directions or sparse features without a
prior vocabulary, then names them from activating inputs; naming validity and stability decide
what it establishes.

\subsubsection{Neuron-level analysis and network dissection}

Network dissection labels each unit with the concept whose mask maximises intersection over union with
its activation map~\cite{bau2017network}, CLIP-Dissect substituting a vision--language
model~\cite{oikarinen2022clipdissect}; the multimodal neurons of CLIP, responding to a concept whether
it is drawn, photographed or written, are the precursor of unit-level multimodal discovery~\cite{goh2021multimodal}. Mid-layer atlases of a pathology encoder show coherent tissue
concepts but dispersed subclasses, pathologist agreement falling from $\kappa$ 0.82 for cancer groups to
0.11 for subclasses~\cite{gustav2026class}; other unit-level work labels mammography neurons, rebuilds
a diagnostic model, probes retinal networks and reads hallucination off language-model
units~\cite{salahuddin2025mammoclip,gong2025concepts,yousefzadeh2023lava,vankadaru2026readable}.
Superposition and non-axis-aligned concepts limit unit
analysis~\cite{elhage2022toy,park2023linear}, a concept is in general carried by a combination of
filters rather than by one~\cite{fong2018net2vec}, and unsupervised factors are not identifiable without
inductive bias or supervision~\cite{locatello2019challenging}, so medical work benchmarks dictionaries (Table~S13).

\subsubsection{Factorisation directions}

Principal components, independent components and non-negative factors give few directions, which in
medical embeddings reflect acquisition and site before pathology (\S\ref{sec:geometry}): among inverted
principal components of pathology-encoder gradients, one stain-colour direction carries 54--69\% of the
tissue-source-site gradient against at most 20\% for any grade component~\cite{howard2024generative}.
Other factorisations recover plausible morphology: concept-bank segmentation beats a
ResNet-50~\cite{gildenblat2024segmentation}$^{\dagger}$ and three pathologist-named HPV-positive
subtypes emerge~\cite{hieromnimon2025analysis}$^{\dagger}$. Carcinoma morphology, retinal characteristics,
breast-cancer archetypes and an infectious-disease proposal are reported
alongside~\cite{qin2026clearhpv,varshney2024generating,beyeler2024comparing,fournier2026prognostic,sizikova2022automatic}.
Activation clustering~\cite{ghorbani2019automating,fel2023a} and sparse decomposition onto a fixed
vocabulary~\cite{bhalla2024interpreting} have no medical instance; dictionary learning grew from
monosemanticity work~\cite{bricken2023monosemanticity,templeton2026scaling}.

\subsubsection{Sparse autoencoders and dictionary learning}

A sparse autoencoder (SAE) reconstructs activations through an overcomplete, sparsely active layer of
candidate monosemantic features~\cite{cunningham2023sparse}. With codes
$a(x)=\sigma(W_e z_\lambda(x)+b_e)$ and reconstruction $\hat z=W_d\,a(x)+b_d$, the dictionary
minimises
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\mathbb{E}_x\big[\|z_\lambda(x)-\hat z(x)\|_2^{2}+\beta\,\|a(x)\|_1\big]\\
&\quad\text{s.t.}\ \|(W_d)_{:,j}\|_2=1\ \forall j,
\end{split}
\label{eq:sae}
\end{equation}
where $(W_d)_{:,j}$ is the $j$th column of the decoder, the direction of feature $j$.
Equation~(\ref{eq:sae}) is the canonical $\ell_1$ objective; the reviewed medical dictionaries replace the
penalty by a fixed active-code count (top-$k$, batch-top-$k$), a gated or jump activation or a matched-pair
crosscoder loss, changing what is held fixed rather than the estimand. Unit norm removes scale degeneracy,
and each $W_d$ column is a feature named from its highest-code inputs, a naming route that need not identify what the feature does, since in vision the maximally activating patches of a dictionary feature can fail a causal test of the same feature~\cite{han2025causal}. Scaling~\cite{gao2024scaling}, better
activations~\cite{rajamanoharan2024jumping} and open dictionaries~\cite{lieberum2024gemma} made SAEs
portable; medical adoption has two waves. Dictionaries that decompose an image--text space jointly, rather than one tower at a time, are a general-domain development the medical corpus has not taken up: group-sparse autoencoders over paired embeddings~\cite{kaushik2026decomposing} and canonical correlation between sparse concept sets~\cite{gordon2026scocca} both address the cross-modal case that the modality gap of \S\ref{sec:geometry} makes hard, and no reviewed medical study uses either.

The first wave trains on \emph{frozen encoders}. Pathologists curate 41 sarcoma concepts on CONCH
embeddings, where the full dictionary reaches 73.7\% balanced accuracy against 77.4\% for the raw
aggregator and 67.0\% for the curated concepts~\cite{bisson2026unsupervised}$^{\dagger}$; restricting the
classifier to the 41 curated features reduced accuracy, but representation, feature count and curation
change together and leakage is flagged, so the cost of naming itself is not isolated. Others take names
from report-distilled descriptions, tissue structures, pathologists or neuropathologists, radiologist
regions or an automated
labeller~\cite{abdulaal2024an,le2024learning,kim2026dissecting,zhao2026compositional,redlich2026explainable,nakka2025mammosae,renzulli2025medsae},
and they compress: ten features recover most downstream CT and MRI performance~\cite{wesp2026sparse} and
a geometry-guided dictionary predicts Alzheimer's conversion~\cite{nerrise2026geosae}. Further variants
appear in Table~S13~\cite{tang2025cxrlanic,sun2025prototypebased,dasdelen2025cytosae,yan2026a,huang2026classguided,chhetri2026when}.
The second wave trains on \emph{residual streams of medical multimodal LLMs}, recovering device,
pathology and longitudinal-change features from a report generator~\cite{bouzid2025insights}, steering
directions on three radiology models (\S\ref{sec:intervention})~\cite{nooralahzadeh2026universal} and a
transferable general suite~\cite{sadanandan2026mechanistically}; domain-specific dictionaries explain more
clinical-activation variance~\cite{oneill2025resurrecting}. No eligible medical study trains a dictionary
across models, across modalities or on model differences. The templates exist in general
work~\cite{thasarathan2025universal,nasirisarvi2025sparc,minder2025overcoming,plantinga2025from}, in
dictionaries over medically fine-tuned language models, task-specific segmentation models and
physiological-signal
models~\cite{tan2026efficient,ahmed2026medseglens,lehnschiler2026mechanistic,duan2026cadence}, and in the
general-vision dictionaries that steer or segment through the visual
stream~\cite{joseph2025steering,pach2025sparse,stevens2025interpretable}, the designs \S\ref{sec:future}
points to.

Across the \Nsae{} core dictionary studies (supplementary Table~S13 lists every
dictionary study in the corpus), every axis that fixes a feature varies:
token, layer, expansion and sparsity. Features are therefore not commensurable, and naming is as
uneven.
Validation of feature meaning uses clinician readers in \Nsaeclin{} studies and structured labels in
\Nsaelabel{}. Only the \Nsaeval{} with clinician or label validation enter the breadth-labelled statement
F7 (\S\ref{sec:findings}); only \Nsaestab{} report reproducibility, none matches features across seeds
and only \Nsaerecon{} quantify reconstruction, so headline claims of ten-feature
compressibility~\cite{wesp2026sparse} and five thousand monosemantic
features~\cite{tang2025cxrlanic} are neither validated nor reproduced.

\subsubsection{Naming, validation and stability}

Automated pipelines name features from activating inputs and score the
predictions~\cite{paulo2024automatically}; the medical instances grade names against reports, labels or
regions, or discover features and then name
them~\cite{bouzid2025insights,abdulaal2024an,haque2026medconcept,nakka2025mammosae,rao2024discover}, none
testing cross-labeller naming. Stability is worse: absorption and splitting persist across widths and
sparsities~\cite{chanin2024a}, seeds share about 30\% of features~\cite{paulo2025sparse}, derived
concepts are adversarially fragile~\cite{li2025evaluating}, and metrics and benchmarks are too unstable to
separate architectures~\cite{chanin2026are,chanin2026synthsaebench}. Few features reproduce in an
event-sequence anchor, where dictionary probes do not beat dense ones~\cite{sainsbury2026sparse}, and
capacity-matched suites are proposed for signal models~\cite{duan2026ecginterpbench}. Named features are
hypotheses, not stable identifiers, though subspace reconstruction survives.


\subsubsection{Summary and discussion}

Names assigned under weak supervision pass their uncertainty to every downstream claim, and encoder
dictionaries largely recover what probing already sees. Two-seed training, cross-cohort feature matching and
any decomposition at the connector output remain open.
