\section{Method families and their medical evidence}
\label{sec:methods}

Following \S\ref{sec:taxonomy}, each subsection defines a family, reviews representative
studies, tabulates the medical evidence and summarises its claims and limits; studies with claims
in two families appear in both. Here ``medical'' means analysing a medical foundation model
(\S\ref{sec:models}); cited general-domain studies supply methods or mechanisms used by medical work. Table~\ref{tab:families} summarises what each family
shows and the tests that strengthen it, and Fig.~\ref{fig:families} draws the ladder of evidence from
observation to concept-specific causal contribution that the formal view below defines. Core studies whose quality note records a defect that undermines the representation-level result (\S\ref{sec:findings}; the judgement is recorded per record in the supplement and counted per finding in supplementary Tables~S9--S11) are marked $^{\dagger}$ where cited singly in this section (a group citation is not marked), so that their results are read with that qualification.


\textbf{Formal view.} In the notation of \S\ref{sec:prelim}, each family can be read as characterising a
functional of the representation $z_\lambda(x)$ at a locus $\lambda$, differing in the functional
examined and in whether the forward pass remains intact. For a multimodal model the state also depends on the prompt $t$, the token position $p$ and, for generated
end-points, the decoding policy, so $z_\lambda(x)$ abbreviates $z_\lambda(x,t,p)$ and $B(x)$ abbreviates
$B(x,t)$ under a fixed decoding rule. A claim about a decoder or connector state is therefore conditional on
the prompt and position at which it was measured, which is why the coding records locus and readout
separately. Structural analysis reports a
statistic $\Phi(\{z_\lambda(x_i)\}_{i=1}^{N})$ of the embedding distribution, unconditionally
or stratified by metadata such as scanner or site, $\Phi(\{z_\lambda(x_i)\}_{i:\,m(x_i)=m})$;
neither decodes a concept. Decoding fits, within a decoder class $\mathcal{G}$ and on a training set
$\mathcal{T}$, the map
\begin{equation}
\hat g=\arg\min_{g\in\mathcal G}\ \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal T|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}\ell\big(g(z_\lambda(x_i)),\,c(x_i)\big)
\label{eq:probe}
\end{equation}
and reports the performance of $\hat g$ on examples held out of $\mathcal T$, which depends on
$\mathcal G$ as much as on $z_\lambda$. Discovery fits a dictionary $D$ and sparse codes $a(x)$ with $z_\lambda(x)\approx D\,a(x)$,
then names its columns. Tracing reports functionals of the unchanged forward pass indexed by
layer, position or component, such as layer-wise decoding of $h^{(\ell)}$ or attention mass on
visual tokens. Intervention replaces $z_\lambda$ by $\tilde z_\lambda$ and reports the
behavioural effect
\begin{equation}
\Delta B=B\big(x;\,z_\lambda\!\leftarrow\!\tilde z_\lambda\big)-B(x),
\label{eq:effect}
\end{equation}
with $\overline{\Delta B}=\mathbb{E}_x[\Delta B(x)]$ its expectation over inputs (and, for generated text, over
the decoding policy), the replacement $\tilde z_\lambda$ being in general itself a function of $x$, $t$ and
$p$; the reviewed studies report sample averages of it. Equation~(\ref{eq:effect})
describes within-run interventions; post-hoc transformations of stored embeddings, parameter editing
and unlearning have their own estimands and subtype-specific end-points (\S\ref{sec:intervention}).
For a steering intervention $\tilde z_\lambda=z_\lambda+\alpha v_c$ along a direction built for concept
$c$, the coding records whether the study reports any matched control against which $\Delta B(\alpha v_c)$
is compared; \S\ref{sec:future} proposes a diagnostic that operationalises the comparison, as a reporting
proposal rather than a standard the reviewed literature was held to. Exceeding matched controls establishes
selective sensitivity to the direction, not semantic validity, which depends on how $v_c$ was obtained.
Erasure carries part of its control in its construction, an affine map removing the linear predictability
of $c$ under a stated metric~\cite{belrose2023leace}, but attributing a behavioural change to it still
requires a sham or rank-matched transformation, and checks that $c$ is not recoverable non-linearly, that
correlated concepts survive and that off-target performance is retained. The paired probe-and-intervention design that the medical corpus lacks (\S\ref{sec:intervention}, \S\ref{sec:future}) follows directly: $v_c$ is the normal of a binary linear probe fitted by~(\ref{eq:probe}) or a dictionary column decoding $c$, and~(\ref{eq:effect}) is reported against matched control directions at the same intervention locus and behavioural end-point.

Eq.~(\ref{eq:probe}) is the canonical decoding case; prompt-similarity scoring recovers $c$ through a fixed similarity structure rather than a fitted $\hat g$, and labelled representational similarity shows only that representation distances covary with concept-labelled distances, without mapping a held-out example to a concept value; both are grouped with decoding by the claim they license, the second as the weakest member (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}). \textbf{Three causal claims.} Three claims of increasing strength follow from~(\ref{eq:effect}).
\emph{Causal sensitivity} means that the output changes when $z_\lambda$ is changed; any
intervention whose effect exceeds its uncertainty over inputs establishes it. \emph{Direction-selective sensitivity}
means that the effect of $v_c$ exceeds that of matched controls (the screening diagnostic proposed in \S\ref{sec:future}). \emph{Concept-specific causal contribution} means that the effect is attributable to the model's representation of $c$ rather than to a merely correlated direction. Necessary but not sufficient conditions for it, beyond matched controls, are validation of $v_c$ through
agreement among several independently constructed directions for the same concept, a
correlated-concept hard-negative control, and an intervention that stays within the empirical activation distribution (the layer's range of activation norms and its principal subspace --- a proximity check, not a test of manifold membership).
Edits far from that distribution can change behaviour for reasons unrelated to $c$; together these checks falsify the simplest alternatives and establish stronger direction-selective sensitivity, consistent with rather than proof of a concept-specific contribution. Identifying it takes a mediation design with a defined exposure, mediator and outcome and the sequential-ignorability assumptions that identify a natural indirect effect~\cite{pearl2001direct,imai2010mediation}, a causal-concept effect estimated under an intervention on the concept itself~\cite{goyal2019cace}, or a causal-abstraction mapping between concept and representation~\cite{geiger2021causal,geiger2023causal}; no medical study in this corpus reaches any of the three. The gap between structure that a representation merely exhibits, structure that an intervention can move, and factors that are causally meaningful is the one Sch\"olkopf \emph{et al.}~\cite{scholkopf2021causal} draw for representation learning generally; the three inferential levels of this review are its measurement-side counterpart. Table~\ref{tab:families} (\S\ref{sec:comparison}) restates this view family by family.

\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{fig6.png}
\caption{From what is encoded to what is used. Observational evidence --- structure, decodability,
discovered features, localisation --- characterises $z_\lambda(x)$ and shows availability, not use. Use
requires an intervention $z_\lambda\to\tilde z_\lambda$ with a pre-specified behavioural end-point
$\Delta B$, and the interventional evidence then climbs three rungs of increasing inferential strength:
\emph{causal sensitivity} ($\Delta B\neq 0$ on held-out inputs; the edited direction may still be
arbitrary), \emph{direction-selective sensitivity} (the effect of $\alpha v_c$ exceeds equal-norm random
and unrelated-concept controls; still not proof of concept-specific causality) and \emph{concept-specific
causal contribution}, for which semantic agreement, hard negatives and an intervention within the
empirical activation distribution are necessary but not sufficient: identifying the contribution takes a
mediation, causal-concept or causal-abstraction design with stated assumptions (\emph{Formal view}), and
no reviewed medical study meets the criterion. The required evidence grows from a behavioural end-point,
to matched controls, to semantic and distributional validation and, finally, an identification design.}
\label{fig:families}
\end{figure*}
